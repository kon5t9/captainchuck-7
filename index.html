I The Captain

Chapter I — Thirty years, then a company

He got tired of explaining
what a professional crew does.

Chuck Brown came up the honest way: deckhand first, engine rooms second, the bridge last. Twenty-three years as a mechanic — diesel to about 1,200 horsepower, AC/DC electrical, hydraulics, cranes — means that when a generator quits at two in the morning, the captain already knows three good reasons and one bad one.

For years the work arrived one boat at a time: a delivery the broker called in on a Friday, a season at the helm of somebody's other office, a refit watched dockside, an owner's boat taught to earn its keep over a summer he wasn't using it. That is how he learned where jobs quietly go wrong — not at sea, but in the paperwork, the watch rotation, and the maintenance interval nobody kept.

So he built the thing he kept recommending to owners: an operation with a captain-service desk, a delivery desk, maintenance programs and checklists — and a bench of vetted captains behind every assignment. A company where the standard is the product, and no boat in it is owned.

The operation ran hard before the pandemic — passages both coasts, seasons in both hemispheres of the calendar, plenty of it on water that doesn't forgive. Then the boats tied up, as everyone's did. In 2026 the company is recommissioned: not a startup with a domain name and a dream, the same crew coming back aboard with the checklists updated.

The centerpiece of the company

The Standard

Every delivery, charter program and management contract starts from the same promise: captains and crews trained to our standard — every credential verified, all in a random-testing consortium. That is not marketing language. It is six checks, done before anybody packs a bag.

  1. 01

    Credentials verified, not photocopied

    Every captain we put aboard holds a USCG merchant mariner credential, checked against the National Maritime Center. Captain Brown's own reads Master of Steam or Motor Vessels, 500 GT / 200 GRT, International — number 2776127. You can look it up yourself; the link is on this page.

  2. 02

    Ratings carry their own papers

    Deck and engineering crew hold Able Seaman credentials. A ticket is the floor, not the ceiling — but it means the Coast Guard has already done a background nobody else has to guess at.

  3. 03

    Random testing, continuously

    The captain and every mariner on the bench sit inside a maritime random drug and alcohol testing consortium. The captain is a non-smoker and finds the whole subject boring, which is the correct professional opinion.

  4. 04

    References actually called

    Former owners, yard managers and head captains get a phone call, not an emailed form. We want the sentence that comes after “technically he finished the job…”. Owner references stay private — ask, and we'll arrange the call.

  5. 05

    Approved on your insurance before we mobilize

    The flow, every time: you contact your insurer, we send our credentials straight to them, and the captain is named and approved on the owner's policy before anyone boards. The company carries no separate policy, because your boat was always covered under your policy anyway — with the right captain named on it.

  6. 06

    Safety tickets current, or they get refreshed

    Vessel Security Officer, fire-fighting, survival craft — the training list further down this page is the company's minimum expectation, and lapses are renewed before a mariner stands a watch for us.

II The Company

Chapter II — What the crews are for

One man is a service.
A standard is a guarantee.

A solo captain, however good, has a hard ceiling: hours in the day. When he sleeps, your passage waits. When he gets influenza, the refit schedule dies in the water. That's a math problem, and it gets solved the same way it is solved on every serious boat — with watches.

So here is the honest arithmetic of the company as it recommissions: one captain you can verify, plus a bench of credentialed hands and relief captains trained to his standard, deployed in three-watch rotations. On a long delivery that is Chuck plus two, nobody tired at the wrong hour. The ambition, stated plainly, is a ten-captain company by the end of the decade — same standard, more watches. You're early, which in this trade is the good position to be in.

There is no fleet to dock, insure, or depreciate. The boats belong to their owners. What the company owns is the way those boats get handled — which is the part that actually matters when the weather turns.

Straight answers

Questions we get

Do you have your own charter fleet?

No, and that's the point. Chuck and his crew work aboard your boat. There is no company fleet to dock, insure and depreciate — just captains and crews hired onto other people's yachts, which is how the service has run for thirty years.

What kind of boats do you handle?

Motor yachts and trawlers, from the routine weekend boat up to about 240 feet as master. Jet drives, direct drives, pods, diesels to around 1,200 hp. Sailboats are somebody else's business; we decline them and never have pretended otherwise.

How does insurance work when you come aboard?

It runs through your policy, the way it should. You contact your insurer, we send our credentials straight to them, and the captain is named and approved on the owner's policy before anybody mobilizes. No separate company policy, no paperwork surprises at the dock.

Who crews a delivery passage?

Chuck plus up to two credentialed hands, running a three-watch rotation so somebody is always rested — the boat moves and nobody falls asleep doing it. Longest passage under his command was about 2,500 miles. The vessel's current survey and seaworthiness get checked before departure; the cosmetics can wait for the detailer.

Why does the website look newer than the captain?

The operation ran hard before the pandemic, paused like everything else did, and is recommissioned for 2026. Today it is one captain with a vetted bench behind him; the stated plan is a ten-captain company, built the slow way. Same standard, same phone number.

Can my boat earn its keep while I'm not using it?

That's the charter department's whole job: running your vessel as a legal charter program under the passenger regulations, with a crew trained to handle paying guests properly, and a revenue split that favors the owner. An idle boat is the most expensive kind.

Do you do pre-purchase surveys?

We do the base inspection and run the sea trial — hard, with a protocol — alongside your chosen surveyor, because we are captains and mechanics, not surveyors. We'll point you toward good surveyors, mechanics and yards; that referral network is being rebuilt after the pause, and we'll tell you exactly where it stands.

Can I speak to previous owners?

Private references are available on request. Owners are never named on a website, and neither are lenders or recovery clients — that's part of the service. Ask when you call, and expect the callback the same day.
